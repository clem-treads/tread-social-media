01 · Strategy

Trust that converts.

Every post either names what our partners are already living, or shows why Tread fixes it. Posts that do neither get cut.

What we sound like

Tread sounds like a foreman who's been on every job site for 20 years. Knows the work. Doesn't waste your time. Tells you what's broken and what to do about it.

Plain language. Technical when precision matters. One sharp opinion per post. One specific number per claim. Dry humor sparingly. If a software vendor could have written it, we didn't.

Why this, not that Operator-fluent voice (we don't explain dispatch to dispatchers) + receipts over hype (sourced numbers, named people, real moments). XBE shouts. HaulHub demos. Tread shows the work.
The causal chain
TRUST → VISIBILITY → VIRALITY

Trust is what we build. Visibility is what compounds. Virality is the outcome we earn by clearly understanding the market, not the outcome we chase by being loud.

01 / Hook-first

The first line decides whether anyone reads the second.

No warm-ups. No "I'm excited to share." One specific detail beats five generic claims. When in doubt, cite the source.

02 / Written for the partner

The dispatcher is the reader. The VP is the buyer.

We write for the dispatcher because the dispatcher is who forwards it to the VP. For family-run producers, the dispatcher and the buyer are often the same person.

03 / Visual + tight hook

Brand always carries a visual. Sameer can ship text-only.

Hook lives above the "see more" fold either way. Specificity over volume.

Voice rule
Tread writes in first-person plural.
Sameer writes in first-person singular.
Both write like operators inside the industry, not consultants explaining it.

Who we're writing for

Contractors, material producers, and haulers (horizontal only). We use the role for the person: hauler, producer, contractor, dispatcher, driver, foreman, estimator, GM. Generic "operator" gets killed. Owner-Operator (the ICP segment) stays.

Language rule

Partner vs Customer

"Tread partners" Referring to our customers (producers, haulers, contractors). Warmer, relational. Default in copy about how we work with them.
"Their customers" The people our partners serve. Haulers serve producers/contractors. Producers serve contractors/owners. Contractors serve project owners/DOTs.
"Customers" (unprefixed) Generic supply-chain context. "The producer bills their customers..." Don't use this for Tread's own customer base.
Use the role When precision matters: "the project owner," "the GC," "the DOT," "the foreman," "the dispatcher." Roles beat relationship-words when a specific person is in the scene.

The test: describing Tread's relationship to them = partners. Describing their own business = customers (theirs).

01
Chapter 01

Five subjects we cover.

Three describe the partner's world (Partner Pain, Hidden Costs, Visibility Gaps). Two describe Tread (Partner Wins, Inside Tread). The system rotates through all five over time so the feed doesn't drift into one shape. Sameer's voice can cover any of them (see the Sameer pane). Click any card to expand.

+
Subject 01 · Partner world

Partner Pain

The day-to-day moments of friction our partners have stopped noticing because they've normalized them. Yellow paper tickets in gloveboxes. 47 unread texts before 7am. 3 hours reconciling scale tickets. Every post names a specific moment and calls out why the industry tolerates it.
Reader reaction"This is exactly what happens to me."
+
Subject 02 · Partner world

Hidden Costs

The dollar or time figure partners are losing and don't see. 50+ days to get paid. Hours reconciling scale tickets. Dead miles. Every post has one defensible number with a named source. We never invent stats.
Reader reaction"This is going on the wall in the dispatch office."
+
Subject 03 · Partner world

Visibility Gaps

The friction between producers, haulers, contractors, and dispatchers that exists because nobody can see the same truth at the same time. Posts name the friction and trace it to the visibility gap. The enemy is opacity, never the partner on the other side.
Reader reaction"We've all been caught on the wrong side of this."
+
Subject 04 · Tread

Partner Wins

Specific outcomes Tread enabled, told as character-driven moments without leading with logos. "The dispatcher who killed 180 paper tickets in 30 days" beats "How [Big Company] Modernized Materials Logistics." Customer naming requires explicit permission (see the Craft pane).
Reader reaction"This is what's possible if I try this."
+
Subject 05 · Tread

Inside Tread

Tread as a multidisciplinary team that chose this problem. Latefa training a foreman team. Tim onboarding a new customer. Sameer in the field. Why a partner should care that this specific team is on this problem.
Reader reaction"These are the people I'd want working on my workflow."
02
Chapter 02

Seven treatments. How we write it.

Subjects are what we talk about. Treatments are how we frame and write each post. Same subject can be written through multiple treatments, so the feed reads fresh across the week even when we're hammering one pain. See the Content Bible for full treatment definitions and examples.

+
Treatment 01 · Narrative

Storytelling

Name a pain that's been accepted as normal. Show what changed when someone fixed it. Knowing, never selling. No philosophical prose. Medium-long, 5-7 short paragraphs. Use when the moment deserves a scene.
Example hook"Most dispatch desks have a Friday afternoon ritual nobody talks about."
+
Treatment 02 · Stat-led

Proof

Clean math, no story. The number does the work. Short, 2–3 lines max. Always paired with a defensible source. Use when the math is the moment.
Example hook"1 in 5 invoices in this industry get disputed. For our customers, it's 1 in 50."
+
Treatment 03 · Tactical

Quick Tip

Advice operators can use right now, with or without Tread. Generous. Saveable. Shareable. Medium length, works as a 5–7-slide carousel. Use to build authority. People follow accounts that teach them something.
Example hook"Three things to lock before you leave the office tonight."
+
Treatment 04 · Update

Behind-the-Scenes

Casual update. "Shipped this week." What we built and why, often triggered by a specific customer ask. Short, 2–3 paragraphs. Use when there's a real product update or moment from the team's work.
Example hook"Shipped this week: the app now reads scale tickets straight from a photo."
+
Treatment 05 · Observation

Industry Truth

One-liner or short observation about the industry that everyone in it knows but no software company has said out loud. Made for shares. Short mode 1–2 lines for shareability; long mode 5–7 lines with the so-what built in.
Example hook"Half of dispatching is texting drivers and praying."
+
Treatment 06 · Human

People

Photo-led human moment. Team in the field, customer visits, real candid scenes. Caption tells the moment plainly. Medium length, photo + 2–4 short paragraphs. Use to humanize the brand and show we ship our work in real rooms with real partners.
Example hook"Latefa spent two days last week sitting next to foremen during their morning briefings."
+
Treatment 07 · Reactive

Trends

Reactive. We don't schedule this lane. When a cultural moment or industry news drops, we ride it within hours. Authentic remix, never forced. Whatever length the moment demands, usually short and visual-led. If it sounds polished or pre-planned, kill it.
Example hook"Sorry we are VERY late to the party. But we came bearing gifts." (Riding the Spotify logo-change moment.)

The hook test

Hooks name a pain that's been accepted as normal, then point at the fix. They never sell. Every first line passes three gates before it ships.

Draft hook → Gate 1 → Gate 2 → Gate 3 → Ship
Gate 01 · Originality Could this opening come from any vendor in our space? If yes, rewrite.
Gate 02 · Specificity Is there a specific number, quote, or scene? If no, add one or rewrite.
Gate 03 · Scroll-stop Would a partner stop scrolling on this line alone? If unsure, rewrite.

Killed on sight

Phrases that disqualify a post immediately. Scan before drafting, scan before shipping.

× "Excited to share..." Wastes the hook.
× "Thrilled to announce..." Always followed by a press release.
× "Honored to partner with..." Corporate ritual. Nobody believes it.
× "At Tread, we believe..." Mission-statement voice. Show, don't preach.
× "In today's fast-paced world..." Doesn't exist for readers in this industry.
× "Game-changer," "revolutionary" Self-aggrandizing. Let partners say it about us.
× "Leverage," "seamless," "robust," "unlock" AI-tell vocabulary. Reads generic, not Tread.
× Em dashes (—) Use periods, commas, or colons.
× `!!!` and ALL CAPS Sameer-only on his own posts. Brand ships clean.
× Any profanity Zero swearing in brand or Sameer posts. Paraphrase partner quotes.
× Outcome-first formulas "100 hours to 4 hours" reads AI-formulaic. Recognize the pain instead.
× Generic "operator" Use the role: hauler, producer, contractor, dispatcher, driver. Owner-Operator (ICP) stays.
× "Feel seen" / "we see you" Soft B2B feels-language. Name the problem. Don't validate the feeling.
× "Reimagine" / "rethink" / "elevate" Vendor-pitch language. Show what changed in the work, don't announce a reframe.
× "Quietly" / "open a door" Spiritual, not operational. A foreman rolls his eyes. Show the fix instead.
× "Journey" / "let's dive in" There is no journey. There's a job site. There's a workflow. There's a fix.
× Emoji / sparkles / ✨ energy Whimsical-fun B2B isn't Tread. Specificity is the emoji.

Chapter 02 · Sourcing

Strong opinions, always sourced.

Every claim anchors to one of three sources. Without one, we sound like outsiders with hot takes.

Three sources of authority

SOURCE 1
Partner quotes & scenarios

From sales calls, CS calls, site visits, customer interviews. Most credible. Cite as "A producer told us..." or "A hauler in [region]..." Always paraphrase carefully, don't repeat verbatim across posts.

SOURCE 2
Industry bodies & publications

Named citations. See list below. Use whenever a strong claim needs external validation.

SOURCE 3
Tread platform data

Real numbers from our platform. "We processed X tons in Q1." Only use when we can defend the number. Never estimate or extrapolate.

Customer naming policy

Default: no customer names in posts.

Posts use anonymized framing ("a producer in Ontario," "the foreman team we trained last week"). Math and outcomes carry the post. Tread is the protagonist, not the customer.

Naming requires three conditions: (1) written or recorded permission from a decision-maker at the customer; (2) the post is reviewed by the customer before publishing if it includes a quote; (3) permission is logged in /04_PERMISSIONS in the asset folder.

We're not closing the door on customer logos forever. We're choosing not to walk through it yet. Twelve months from now, when brand strength is established, that conversation gets easier.

Industry sources to cite

Standard citation sources for materials, hauling, and construction logistics. Verify any stat against the source before publishing.

NSSGA
National Stone, Sand & Gravel Assoc.

Quarterly aggregates demand reports. The default authority on aggregates production and trends.

NAPA
National Asphalt Pavement Assoc.

Annual industry data on asphalt production, paving, employment.

NRMCA
National Ready Mixed Concrete Assoc.

Concrete production volume, fleet data, industry surveys.

Infrastructure spending, federal/state DOT budgets, project pipelines.

Contractor workforce surveys, payment terms research, construction economics.

07 · Reference

When something feels unclear.

Click any question to expand. Closed by default so you can scan headlines first.

01Subject vs treatment, what's the difference?
Subject is what we talk about. Treatment is how we frame and write it. Every post needs both. Subject = Hidden Costs, Treatment = Proof → stat-led 2-3 line post. Subject = Hidden Costs, Treatment = Storytelling → 5-7 paragraph Friday reconciliation narrative. Same subject, two completely different posts.
02Why five subjects and seven treatments?
Five subjects keep the feed focused on what matters. Seven treatments keep the same subject reading fresh across the week. Same Hidden Costs subject through Proof + Storytelling + Quick Tip = three native posts that don't read like the same idea three times.
03Why isn't Industry Pulse a treatment of its own?
Trends is the treatment. Industry Pulse is the input feed that surfaces what to apply the Trends treatment to. A news story about NSSGA Q1 demand isn't a "Pulse post." It's a Hidden Costs subject treated as Proof, sourced from the news cycle.
04How do brand voice and Sameer's voice compound?
A theme runs across both tracks in the same week, from different angles. Sameer's voice can cover any of the 5 subjects, not just "CEO topics." Same pain, four angles, five days. Repetition without redundancy.
05What happens when a feature ships?
Product fills out the Tier 1 intake form (3 min) the day it ships. Routing sorts it into Skip, Bundle, Social, or Campaign. The feature is never the hook. The partner pain it solves is. Major launches carry hard CTAs.
06What if a post fits more than one subject?
Pick the subject that captures the strongest angle, not all the angles. If the topic is strong enough to do all three, that's a feature campaign. Split into three posts across the week and earn a compounding theme.
07Invent vs wait for real numbers?
Never invent. Always wait. If no defensible number: cite a range with source named, reframe around time/count, or kill the post. Inventing once destroys trust for every real number we'll ever post.
08No industry source for the claim?
Three options. (1) Replace with a partner quote, often stronger than industry-body-sourced for our audience. (2) Reframe around a Tread platform number we can defend. (3) Soften from stat to observation ("we've heard this from dozens of producers"). If none work, the post isn't ready.
09How does social drive MQLs if 60% have no CTA?
60% no-CTA builds surface area (follows, profile visits). 30% soft-CTA captures intent (comments, DMs, downloads). 10% hard-CTA converts at peak intent. Each tier feeds the next. Track in HubSpot: profile visit → content download → demo booking.
10Visuals on LinkedIn vs X?
LinkedIn, FB, IG → Tread brand always carries a visual. Sameer's posts can ship text-only when he doesn't have a photo. X is text-native, short text performs because the platform expects it. Stat cards work for brand when no photo is available.

The shipping checklist

  • Hook test: Does the first line make the reader want the second?
  • Specificity test: Is there one sticky detail (quote, number, scene)?
  • Source test: Is every strong claim backed by partner, industry body, or platform data?
  • Opinion test: Could a competitor have written this? If yes, rewrite.
  • Voice test: First-person plural for brand, first-person singular for Sameer.
  • Partner language test: Did we say "operator" anywhere? Change to partner or role.
  • Fluff test: Any "excited to share," "thrilled to," "honored to"? Kill them.
  • Number test: Every number defensible. None invented.
  • Visual test: Brand post, photo, graphic, carousel, or video ready? CEO post, photo or clip preferred; if text-only, does the hook carry it?
  • Length test: Within the channel's character range?
  • CTA test: Right CTA tier for the bucket?
  • Approval test: If CEO post, has Sameer reviewed it?

Trust → Visibility → Virality.

The five components

What gets a post from idea to published consistently every week. Install in this order.

+
Component 01 · Cadence

Monday huddle (15 min)

Same agenda every week. (1) Pick 3 subjects for the week from the 5 available. Rotate so we cover all 5 over time and hit each product cycle stage over 4–6 weeks. (2) Assign treatments for each subject from the 7 available, based on what we have material for. (3) Map to channels and voice. (4) Confirm visuals + block draft time. Output: a populated week. 3 subjects, ~6 posts, treatments assigned, channels routed, visuals confirmed.
+
Component 02 · Roles

Hat-switching, one person

Four hats, one owner. Strategist (Monday huddle, 30 min/wk). Writer (Tue/Wed drafting, 2–3 hrs/wk). Editor (Wed/Thu cold review 24h later, 30 min/wk). Publisher (Thu/Fri scheduling, 30 min/wk). Sameer drafts or voice-memos his own posts. Clem edits lightly for clarity but never rewrites his voice. He signs off before publish.
+
Component 03 · Library

Asset folder

One Google Drive folder. Rule: nothing gets used in a post if it's not in the folder, tagged, and dated. Structure: /01_PHOTOS (team_in_field, product_screenshots, industry_scenes, brand_assets) · /02_VIDEOS · /03_AUDIO · /04_PERMISSIONS (customer consents) · /05_PUBLISHED (final post + visual archived by date). Monday huddle starts with: "what's in the folder we haven't used yet?"
+
Component 04 · Workflow

Draft → review → ship → archive

Draft (Tue/Wed): every post for the week in one doc. One section per post with subject, treatment, channel, voice, visual, copy. Review (Wed/Thu) cold 24h later. Check the hook holds, voice is consistent, no invented moments, no formula feel. Schedule (Thu/Fri) into Buffer or native schedulers. Ship + archive (Mon–Sat): drop final copy + visual into /05_PUBLISHED. Posting times: LinkedIn 8–9 AM ET weekdays · FB 9–11 AM ET · IG 11 AM–1 PM ET · X 10 AM–2 PM ET · Saturday tips 9–10 AM ET.
+
Component 05 · Measurement

One spreadsheet, updated weekly

Columns: Post ID · Date · Channel · Subject (5 options) · Treatment (7 options) · Voice (Tread/Sameer) · Hook (first line, for pattern analysis) · Engagement (likes, comments, reshares, saves) · Profile visits · Inbound DMs · Demos attributed (ask new demos where they heard about Tread) · Notes. Weekly review (Fri, 15 min): which subject pulled, which treatment performed, which channel converted. Monthly review (last Fri, 30 min): on pace to 143 MQLs/month? Which subjects over-index on engagement vs MQLs?

Chapter 04 · When things break

Edge cases. Decided in advance.

Real Mondays are messy. These are the scenarios we've pre-decided so the system doesn't stall.

If
Sameer hasn't approved at 48h
Pull the post. Swap in a brand post from the queue (Partner Pain × Storytelling is the default fallback). Move the Sameer draft to next week's huddle.
If
Industry Pulse has nothing this week
Pulse stays empty. Don't force it. Trends treatment skips; we run two extra evergreen Storytelling or Proof posts instead.
If
Customer pulls naming consent mid-week
Take the post down within the hour. Replace with anonymized version ("a producer in Ontario") if the math still carries. Log the pull in /04_PERMISSIONS.
If
A post fails the hook test the day it ships
Don't ship to make the slot. Skip the day. A missed post beats a forgettable one. Note in Friday review.
If
Sameer voice memo intake skipped
His account ships at lower volume that week (1 post instead of 2). Don't manufacture material to fill the slot.
If
A stat can't be verified before publishing
Reframe around partner quote, Tread platform number, or kill the post. Inventing destroys trust for every future number.
