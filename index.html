01 · Overview

Two jobs. Every post serves one.

Tread's social media has one purpose: make the brand the industry talks about. Every post either makes our partners feel seen, or makes Tread the company they trust to fix what they're seeing. Posts that do neither get cut.

01 / Hook-first

The first line decides whether anyone reads the second.

No warm-ups. No "I'm excited to share." One specific detail beats five generic claims.

02 / Opinion, sourced

Strong opinions, backed by partner quotes or industry data.

Take a side, but always cite the source. Strong claims without sources sound like outsiders.

03 / Written for the partner

The dispatcher is the reader. The VP is the buyer.

We write for the dispatcher because the dispatcher is who forwards it to the VP.

04 / Visual + tight hook

Every post has a visual. The hook is one line, not a paragraph.

LinkedIn, FB, IG are visual feeds. No image, no scroll-stop. Hook lives above the "see more" fold.

Voice rule
Tread writes in first-person plural, with explicit ownership of opinions.
Sameer writes in first-person singular, with explicit ownership of doubt.

The audience, named

Our partners are contractors, material producers, and haulers. When describing a relationship, we say partner. When describing what someone does, we use the role: hauler, producer, contractor, dispatcher, driver. "Operator" is what we're moving away from.

02 · The Six Buckets

Six buckets. One job each.

Three buckets are about the industry and the partner. Three are about Tread and its people. Half the calendar is "we see you." Half is "here's who we are." Neither half works alone.

Bucket 01 · Track A (Brand)

Partner Pain

The day-to-day moments of friction our partners have stopped noticing because they've normalized them. Yellow paper tickets in gloveboxes. 47 unread texts before 7am. 3 hours reconciling scale tickets. Every post names a specific moment and takes a position on why the industry tolerates it.
Reader reaction"This is exactly what happens to me."
Bucket 02 · Track A (Brand)

Hidden Costs

The dollar or time figure partners are losing and don't see. 50+ days to get paid. Hours reconciling scale tickets. Dead miles. Every post has one defensible number with a named source. We never invent stats.
Reader reaction"I need to send this to my CFO."
Bucket 03 · Track A (Brand)

Industry Tensions

The fights between producers, haulers, contractors, and dispatchers that nobody discusses publicly. Producers think haulers are unreliable. Haulers think producers don't pay on time. Both are right. Posts name the tension and take a side. The contrarian engine.
Reader reaction"I can't believe someone finally said this."
Bucket 04 · Track A (Brand)

Partner Wins

Specific partners doing specific things Tread enabled, told as character-driven stories without leading with logos. "The dispatcher who killed 180 paper tickets in 30 days" beats "How [Big Company] Modernized Materials Logistics." Lower legal bar, faster to ship.
Reader reaction"This is what's possible if I try this."
Bucket 05 · Track B (CEO)Sameer

Sameer's Take

The CEO's voice on his personal LinkedIn. What he's hearing from partners. What surprised him on a sales call. What he learned and what it changed. First-person singular, conversational, opinion-forward. Highest-leverage channel Tread has.
Reader reaction"This guy actually gets it."
Bucket 06 · Track A (Brand)

Inside Tread

Tread as a multidisciplinary team that chose this problem. Hiring stories. Team gatherings. Individual team features. Operating-model posts (sales ride-alongs, in-person CS). Every post answers: why should a partner care that this specific team is on this problem?
Reader reaction"These are the people I want building my software."

Two tracks. Coordinated, not parallel.

Tread's brand account is the workhorse. Sameer's personal account is the amplifier. CEO posts get 5–10x the reach of brand posts on LinkedIn for early-stage B2B, so this isn't optional — it's the highest-leverage channel we have.

How feature releases flow into socials

Every shipped feature goes through one of four paths. The Tier 1 intake form (Product fills out in 3 minutes the day a feature ships) determines which path.

Path 01 Skip Backend or minor feature. No social post.
Path 02 Bundle Quality-of-life feature. Aggregated into a monthly "What we shipped" post in Inside Tread bucket.
Path 03 Social Meaningful feature. 1-3 posts in a single week, slotted into Partner Pain → Hidden Costs → Partner Wins. Feature is never the hook.
Path 04 Campaign (Tier 2) Major launch. Drives the week's theme across both tracks. 4-5 brand + 2-3 CEO posts over 1-2 weeks. Sales and CS enablement assets ship in parallel.

05 · Visual + Copy Rules

Every post has a visual. No exceptions on LinkedIn, FB, IG.

LinkedIn, Facebook, and Instagram are visual feeds. Text-only posts get scrolled past. Every post on these channels ships with a visual — photo, stat card, pull-quote card, carousel, or short video. X is the only channel where text-only is acceptable.

The visual hierarchy

Visual typeEffortWhen to use
Photo (real)LowestSite visits, team gatherings, customer training. Always preferred when available.
Short video (15-30 sec)LowField reports. CEO field updates. Phone clips beat polished video.
Stat cardMediumHidden Costs posts. Single number on yellow/black background.
Pull-quote cardMediumIndustry Tensions, Sameer's Take. One sentence, max 15 words on card.
Carousel (3-7 slides)HighPartner Wins (before/after). Feature explainers. Sparingly.
Document upload (PDF)HighSoft-CTA posts where the asset is the value (benchmarks, guides).

Copy lengths by channel

Best-practice lengths for each platform. Hook always comes first — the line above the "see more" fold is what gets read.

ChannelLengthFormat rules
LinkedIn Brand 800-1,300 chars Hook on line 1 (5-12 words, no fluff). Blank line. Body in 5-7 short paragraphs. White space matters. End with opinion or open question.
LinkedIn Sameer 600-1,000 chars Hook on line 1. One idea per line where possible. Conversational. Shorter than brand. White space > word count.
Facebook 400-800 chars Photo-first. Warmer tone. Longer prose ok but split into short paragraphs. Reads like a person talking, not a brand broadcasting.
X / Twitter 1-2 sentences Hook line or a single number. Often the LinkedIn hook lifted verbatim. Repost CEO snippets. High frequency, low effort.
Instagram 1-3 sentences Photo is the post. Caption supports it. Never long-form. If you need to explain it, use a carousel instead.

The hook test

Before any post ships, the first line gets tested against three questions:

TestPass / Fail
Could this opening be from any vendor in our space?If yes — rewrite.
Does it use a specific number, quote, or scene?If no — rewrite.
Would a partner stop scrolling on this line alone?If unsure — rewrite.

What gets killed on sight

Phrases that disqualify a post immediately. They are corporate fluff and trigger scroll-past:

Killed phraseWhy
"Excited to share..."Tells the reader nothing. Wastes the hook.
"Thrilled to announce..."Same. Always followed by a press release.
"Honored to partner with..."Corporate ritual. Nobody believes it.
"At Tread, we believe..."Generic mission-statement voice. Show, don't preach.
"In today's fast-paced world..."Doesn't exist for readers in this industry.
"Game-changer," "revolutionary"Self-aggrandizing claims. Let partners use these words about us.
06 · Sourcing + Industry Pulse

Strong opinions, always sourced.

Tread takes strong positions. But every strong position is grounded in evidence: a partner quote from a real conversation, an industry-published stat with a named source, or a Tread platform number we can defend. Without that, we sound like outsiders with hot takes.

The three sources of authority

Every post anchors to at least one of these. If none apply, the post needs rewriting or killing.

SOURCE 1
Partner quotes & scenarios

From sales calls, CS calls, site visits, customer interviews. Most credible. Cite as "A producer told us..." or "A hauler in [region]..." Always paraphrase carefully — don't repeat verbatim across posts.

SOURCE 2
Industry bodies & publications

Named citations. See list below. Use whenever a strong claim needs external validation.

SOURCE 3
Tread platform data

Real numbers from our platform. "We processed X tons in Q1." Only use when we can defend the number. Never estimate or extrapolate.

Industry sources to cite

Standard citation sources for materials, hauling, and construction logistics. Verify any stat against the source before publishing.

NSSGA
National Stone, Sand & Gravel Assoc.

Quarterly aggregates demand reports. The default authority on aggregates production and trends.

NAPA
National Asphalt Pavement Assoc.

Annual industry data on asphalt production, paving, employment.

NRMCA
National Ready Mixed Concrete Assoc.

Concrete production volume, fleet data, industry surveys.

ARTBA
American Road & Transportation Builders

Infrastructure spending, federal/state DOT budgets, project pipelines.

AGC
Associated General Contractors

Contractor workforce surveys, payment terms research, construction economics.

ENR
Engineering News-Record

Industry's primary trade publication. Cite ENR rankings, headlines, market analysis.

Trade pubs
Aggregates Manager · Pit & Quarry · Roads & Bridges

Industry-specific publications. Operator-facing. Good for context and headlines.

Levelset / Procore
Construction Payments Report (annual)

Source for "50+ days to get paid" and related payment-cycle stats. The most cited construction payments research.

EIA
U.S. Energy Information Administration

Weekly diesel and gasoline prices. Use for fuel surcharge posts, dead miles cost posts.

The sourcing rule

If a post makes a strong claim with no source from any of those three categories, rewrite or kill it. Sourcing is what separates "operators we belong with" from "outsiders with opinions."

Industry Pulse — riding the news cycle

Industry Pulse is not a 7th bucket. It's a sourcing lens layered on top of the six buckets. 1-2 posts per week should respond to a current news cycle — federal infrastructure spending updates, NSSGA quarterly releases, a competitor's funding announcement, a diesel price spike. Pulse posts get 3-5x the engagement of evergreen posts because the LinkedIn algorithm rewards timeliness.

The Pulse workflow

WhenWhat happens

Friday calendar draft Pulse posts get slotted into the week. Usually replace an evergreen Partner Pain or Industry Tensions slot.
Monday-Friday execution Pulse posts ship within 48-72 hours of the news event whenever possible. Speed matters more than polish for these.

07 · CTA Architecture

60% no CTA. 30% soft. 10% hard.

If every post asks for a demo, none of them work. If no post ever asks, we have a popular feed and no pipeline. The right balance is tiered: most posts build trust without asking, a smaller subset offers a low-friction next step, and a minority route directly to sales.

60%
No CTA
Trust and reach

Partner Pain, Industry Tensions, most Sameer posts, most Inside Tread. The point is reach, recognition, and shareability. A CTA here kills credibility.

Funnel signal: follows, profile visits, comment quality.

30%
Soft CTA
Low-friction next step

Hidden Costs (download our benchmarking guide), Partner Wins (DM us for the full story), Inside Tread (DM us for an on-site rollout). The ask is small and value-forward.

Funnel signal: comments → DMs, gated downloads, MQL handoff.

10%
Hard CTA
Demo or call request

Feature launch campaigns (Tier 2). High-intent Partner Wins. Posts targeted at mid-funnel audiences already nodding along. Direct, specific.

Funnel signal: demo bookings, contact form submissions, SQLs.

10 · Appendix

How it all fits together.

A reference page for when something feels unclear. Why we built the system this way, how the pieces feed each other, and what to do when a post doesn't fit cleanly into a bucket.

Q1

Why six buckets and not more or fewer?

Six is the number that covers every story Tread needs to tell without overlap. Three buckets describe the partner's world (Partner Pain, Hidden Costs, Industry Tensions). Three buckets describe Tread (Partner Wins, Sameer's Take, Inside Tread). If we had three, we'd lose nuance. If we had nine, content would land in multiple buckets and we'd burn time filing. Six is the sweet spot.

Q2

Why isn't Industry Pulse a 7th bucket?

Because Pulse is a sourcing lens, not a content type. A news story about NSSGA Q1 demand becomes a Partner Pain post or a Hidden Costs post — same buckets, just sourced from the news cycle instead of cold. Making it a bucket would mean Pulse posts have to be one thing. They don't. They're whatever bucket the news fits into, framed for timeliness.

Q3

How does the brand voice and the CEO voice compound?

A theme runs across both tracks in the same week, from different angles. Example: Tread brand posts about a partner pain (Monday). Sameer posts his personal reaction to the same pain from a sales call (Wednesday). Tread brand posts the math behind the pain (Wednesday). Sameer posts a vulnerability-plus-take reflection on the week's theme (Friday). The reader sees the same pain from four angles in five days. Repetition without redundancy.

Q4

What happens when a feature ships?

Product fills out the Tier 1 intake form (3 minutes) the day the feature ships. Based on field 5, the feature is routed: Skip, Bundle, Social, or Campaign. The feature is never the hook of a post — the partner pain it solves is. The feature shows up in the last third of the post or in the comments. Major launches (Campaign) carry hard CTAs.

Q5

What if a post fits more than one bucket?

Pick the bucket that captures the strongest angle, not all the angles. A post about a partner saving 3 hours a day could be Partner Pain (lead with the pain), Hidden Costs (lead with the math), or Partner Wins (lead with the partner). Each version is a different post. If the topic is strong enough to do all three, that's a feature campaign — split into three posts across the week and we earn a compounding theme.

Q6

Why doesn't Inside Tread have its own track or account?

Because it's part of Tread's brand story, not separate from it. Hiring posts, team gatherings, and operating-model content live alongside partner pain content because they answer the same reader question from different sides. Pain content answers "do you understand my world?" Inside Tread content answers "are you the people who can fix it?" Both come from Tread's brand account.

Q7

What's the rule for when to invent vs. wait for real numbers?

Never invent. If we don't have a defensible number, we either (a) use a range with the source named ("haulers wait 50+ days to get paid" — Levelset 2024), (b) reframe the post around time or count instead of cost, or (c) kill the post and replace with a Partner Pain post that doesn't need a number. Inventing a number once destroys trust for every real number we'll ever post.

Q8

What if we don't have an industry source for the claim?

Three options: (1) Replace the claim with a partner quote — partner-sourced authority is often stronger than industry-body-sourced for our audience. (2) Reframe around a Tread platform number we can defend. (3) Soften the claim from a stat to an observation ("we've heard this from dozens of producers" instead of "76% of producers"). If none of those work, the post isn't ready.

Q9

How does social drive MQLs if 60% of posts have no CTA?

The 60% no-CTA posts build the surface area — follows, profile visits, recognition. The 30% soft-CTA posts capture intent — comments, DMs, gated downloads. The 10% hard-CTA posts convert at the moment of highest intent. Each tier feeds the next. Track in HubSpot: profile visit → content download → demo booking. The funnel is the architecture, not the individual post.

Q10

What's the rule for visuals on LinkedIn vs. X?

LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram: visual required, no exceptions. Text-only posts get scrolled past on visual feeds. X is the exception — short text posts perform well there because the platform is text-native. For LinkedIn Sameer, a real photo or short video clip is always preferred over a designed graphic. For Tread brand, a designed stat or pull-quote card is fine when no photo is available.

Q11

When does this playbook get revisited?

After 30 days of execution. We'll look at: which buckets performed best on which channels, whether the Pulse workflow produced lift, whether the CTA architecture produced measurable MQL volume, whether visuals correlated with engagement as expected. Tighten or rewrite based on real data.

The shipping checklist

  • Hook test: Does the first line make the reader want the second?
  • Specificity test: Is there one sticky detail (quote, number, scene)?
  • Source test: Is every strong claim backed by partner, industry body, or platform data?
  • Opinion test: Could a competitor have written this? If yes, rewrite.
  • Voice test: First-person plural for brand, first-person singular for Sameer.
  • Partner language test: Did we say "operator" anywhere? Change to partner or role.
  • Fluff test: Any "excited to share," "thrilled to," "honored to"? Kill them.
  • Number test: Every number defensible. None invented.
  • Visual test: Photo, graphic, carousel, or video — and is it actually ready?
  • Length test: Within the channel's character range?
  • CTA test: Right CTA tier for the bucket?
  • Approval test: If CEO post, has Sameer reviewed it?
